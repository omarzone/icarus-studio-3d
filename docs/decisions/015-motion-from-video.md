# 015 · Motion from video: pose keypoints, limb directions and auto-rig

**Context.** Copying a reference video needs a pose estimate per frame, a way to map a person onto a body built from primitives, a reward that trains against it and an imported mesh that becomes a body. 3D pose and joint retargeting need models and skeleton data the studio does not have.

**Decision.** The app samples a video at 8 frames per second (up to 12 s) and the engine sends each frame to the `pose` capability through the generic connection, asking for 14 keypoints per person in a fixed JSON schema (`PoseEstimate`, validated, one retry). Each limb between two keypoints keeps its direction in the picture's plane; a limb maps onto a body part by name and side, and those angles are frozen in the task (`TaskSpec.motion`) by `set_motion_reference`. The `imitation` family pays `track_motion`, exp(-(angle error / 0.6 rad)²) averaged over the tracked limbs, and the brain sees where in the clip it is. Auto-rig fits a humanoid skeleton to a standing mesh from horizontal slices (two legs, crotch, torso width, hands) with body proportions where slices cannot tell, builds the vault humanoid's layout sized from it, and binds every triangle to one part for drawing.

**Consequences.** Without a pose model nothing is tracked and the app says so. Depth, twist, fingers, toes and bent rest poses do not transfer; a side view suits bodies whose hinges pitch forward and back. Comparisons and exports read the latest evaluation episode limb by limb the same way the reward does. Skins never touch physics, so rebinding keeps a trained brain.
